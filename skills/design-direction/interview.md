# Interview

Three to five questions per round. Prefer questions the person can answer from experience over
questions about taste. When an answer is an adjective ("modern", "clean", "premium"), ask what it
looks like in a thing they have seen. When an open question draws a blank, offer concrete options
to react to: named products, pictures, opposite pairs.

When a scale answer is uncertain, or the person asks to see the difference, build that axis as
the directions in step 4: hold every other answer fixed and vary only that one.

## Round 1 — the product and its people

- Who uses this, in what situation, and how often? What is on their desk or in their hands?
- What should someone feel or know five seconds after it opens?
- Is it a tool people work in, a page that has to convince, or both?

## Round 2 — the world

First ask whose world it is. A tool for the people who run a product lives in the operators'
world (its records, pipelines, health), not the customers' field; ask about that one.

- What does this field work with every day: materials, places, instruments, documents, terms of
  art? Name objects, not moods.
- Which of those would an insider recognise instantly, and an outsider never think of?
- What are the field's own visual conventions (forms, markings, charts, signage, uniforms)?

## Round 3 — personality

Place the product on each scale, 1 to 5, and say why for the extremes:

- quiet — loud
- technical — warm
- instrumental — editorial
- dense — airy
- classic — experimental
- serious — playful

## Round 4 — references

- Three products, brands or pages you admire, and the one thing you take from each.
- Three this must never resemble: competitors, look-alikes, styles you are tired of.
- Any colour, typeface or shape that is already taken in this field?
- Which template or AI-made look do you already see everywhere in this field? Show them
  [defaults.md](defaults.md) and let them mark the ones that bother them most.

## Round 5 — the one thing

- How recognisably yours must this surface be: low (a clean tool is enough), medium, high (it has
  to be unmistakable)? Set it per surface; an internal tool and a landing page rarely share it.
- If people remember one visual idea from this product, what should it be?
- Where does the brand get to speak loudly, and where must it stay out of the way?

Close by stating back the world, the personality and the anti-references in one paragraph, and
correct it until the person agrees.
